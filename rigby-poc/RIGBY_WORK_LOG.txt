1.2 THE BODY-PART MOVEMENT VOCABULARY
-------------------------------------
rom.v1.json says how far each of the rig's 156 DOFs may rotate. It does not say
what any of them are for. The capability description that did exist -- FINGERS,
SEGMENTS, HAND_SHAPES -- is Python literals: 297 of the 307 float literals in
primitives.py are invisible to test_no_hardcoded_thresholds by construction,
because that guard is AST-defined as a literal in a comparison and hand-shape
curls live in dict values.

config/body_parts.v1.json, generated from rom.v1.json by
scripts/generate_body_parts.py: 30 parts covering all 52 bones, 304 named moves,
8 timed sequences. Each finger is one part rather than three hidden segments.

The decision that makes the two halves agree: a move is a SIGNED FRACTION of a
DOF's own ROM range, never an angle. {"flexion": 0.6} is 60% of the way to that
joint's typical flexion limit, so an out-of-range pose is unnameable rather than
merely rejected.

Rotations compose through analysis.anatomy.frame.compose -- the same anatomical
frames rom_checks decomposes with -- and the round trip is asserted over every
move, so the vocabulary cannot drift into a different basis and be bounds-checked
afterwards.

Validation caught a real gap: clavicle, elbow, knee, ankle and toes each had a
DOF with a ROM entry and NO move that could reach it. The catalog now refuses to
load in that state; all 30 parts reach all three DOFs.

2.1 THE MOCAP VELOCITY DISCOVERY  (the most important finding of the session)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    mocap body moved 0.05 m per 2 ms step (= 25 m/s of apparent motion)
      cvel MuJoCo reports for the MOCAP body : [0. 0. 0. 0. 0. 0.]
      cvel MuJoCo reports for a FREE body    : [0. 0. 0. 0. 0. -0.0981]

A mocap body reports ZERO velocity however fast it is teleported. Contact
tangential velocity is computed from cvel, so a mocap surface is permanently
stationary to the friction solver: it can resist an object sliding but can never
carry one. A hand built from mocap bodies can only move an object by caging it.

Proven on an otherwise identical two-jaw gripper:

    jaws driven as        lift        opposing contact   hold contacts
    mocap                 0.0000 m    0.00               table only
    welded dynamic        0.1080 m    1.00               both jaws, 450/450

This invalidates the earlier diagnosis. "The grasp does not close" was measuring
a simulation structurally incapable of a friction grasp. Those rejected
candidates were failing a gate the rig could not pass regardless of the motion.

2.2 THE DRIVE FIX
-----------------
Collision bodies are now dynamic bodies welded to mocap targets, not mocap bodies
themselves. The weld is hand-to-target and never hand-to-object; the block keeps
its free joint and is moved only by contact.

Second bug found doing it: MuJoCo captures a weld's relative pose AT COMPILE
TIME, so bodies declared away from the origin track their targets at exactly that
offset forever.

    palm tracking error before relpose fix : 1010.00 mm  (= the support height)
    after pinning relpose to identity      :    0.544 mm mean, 4.138 mm max
                                               (during close / lift / hold)

Well inside the 1-3 mm interference a grip needs.

Provenance states this honestly: object_weld_used=False still holds, with
tracking_weld_used=True recorded alongside rather than hiding behind a bare
weld_used=False.

2.3 BONE-DRIVEN PHYSICS PORTED FROM angel/pick-up-box
-----------------------------------------------------
simulate_embodied_grasp: 16 collision bodies (one palm, three segments per digit)
whose transforms are recomputed every step from the exact compiled bones the
renderer draws. No cartesian_hand, no gripper actuator, no copied proxy grasp.

Grab compilation is now two-pass: author the humanoid armature first, then drive
MuJoCo from those frames and copy the free-body trajectory back. Previously
physics ran BEFORE the hand was posed at all -- the object's motion was decided
before the thing supposedly moving it existed.

Opposition is measured from real contact normals (antiparallel within cos <=
-0.70, both carrying >= 0.05 N) rather than from "the thumb is touching and at
least one finger is touching". The old rule rejected valid palm-opposed power
grasps and accepted a thumb resting on an object with nothing opposing it.

Contact compliance softened from solref 0.004 to 0.010. Under a near-rigid model
the discretised approach crossed its millimetre of clearance inside one 2 ms step
and the object was launched by a 128 N impulse instead of being gripped. A 10 ms
time constant models the compliance of skin and soft tissue.

2.6 TALMY MOTION SITUATIONS
---------------------------
Source: Talmy, "Semantics and Syntax of Motion" (1975), pp. 181-238, read from
the PDF in Downloads. A motion situation partitions into FIGURE, GROUND, PATH and
MOTION -- a closed verb set of MOVE and BEL, where a located state is the
limiting case. MANNER enters from outside and adjoins the verb, giving Mm.

This inverts the pipeline's direction of travel. Today the compiler authors body
motion and the object's trajectory is whatever falls out; nothing states what the
object was SUPPOSED to do. A motion situation states exactly that.

config/talmy_paths.v1.json -- the closed set: OFF, ONTO, INTO, TO, AWAY_FROM,
ACROSS, DOWN, AT. Closed by construction because that is Talmy's actual claim.

    'pick up box'                  -> N(block) V(MOVE+GRASPED) Pl(OFF)
    'hold the block'               -> N(block) V(BEL+GRASPED) Pl(AT)
    'put the block on the ladder'  -> N(block) V(MOVE) Pl(ONTO) N(ladder)
    'wave hello'                   -> None

Building the contour caught a bug in the first version: ONTO targeted 2.14 m, the
top of the ladder's BOUNDING BOX. It now resolves through the ladder's declared
weight-bearing sockets to a rung at 1.91 m. The scene already said where it
affords support.

Scoring the current pickup against its own specification:

    the block must rise    0.1200 m
    it actually moved      0.2828 m
    endpoint error         0.3072 m
    block y: 1.0500 -> peak 1.0604 -> end 1.0500
    lateral drift          0.2828 m

The block ends at its STARTING HEIGHT, 28 cm sideways. The hand swats it off the
table.

2.7 CONTOUR FITTING
-------------------
contour_fit.py -- a pattern search descending the contour loss. Pattern search
because the objective is not differentiable (an infinitesimal parameter change
can flip whether a contact happens at all) and because the budget is tens of
evaluations, not thousands: one evaluation is a full compile plus a full MuJoCo
pass.

    loss 0.5485 -> 0.3709   (32% reduction, 36 evaluations, 197 s)
    lateral knock-off 0.283 m -> 0.172 m

Safety properties, asserted: never returns worse than baseline, respects budget,
reports exhaustion as exhaustion rather than convergence, stays in bounds,
deterministic.

It will not solve the grasp. No setting of six knobs makes a hand that drives
20 mm into the block lift it.

2.8 THE MOTION STATE CONTROLLER
-------------------------------
config/motion_states.v1.json -- seven states, each naming what it solves, what it
holds inert, which gates mean anything there, and where to look.

    0. reach     solves arm,forearm        gates -                          gaze object
    1. preshape  solves wrist,palm,digits  gates palm_surface_orientation   gaze object
    2. contact   solves arm,forearm,wrist  gates socket_alignment, palm     gaze object
    3. close     solves digits             gates digit_chain_and_opposition gaze grasp
    4. lift      solves arm,forearm        gates embodied_contact_dynamics  gaze object
    5. hold      solves -                  gates embodied_contact_dynamics  gaze object
    6. recover   solves arm,forearm        gates embodied_contact_dynamics  gaze forward

Repairs are scoped to the region the failing state actually solves:

    before: 45-94 s per candidate
    after:  16 s, 3 compiles -- same callbacks, same final verdict

Roughly 4x. But the correctness half matters more: during lift the digits are
inert BECAUSE THEY ARE HOLDING THE OBJECT. The old controller would re-solve them
there, changing the grip that is at that moment carrying the block.

Safety property, in code and tests: scoping may NEVER empty the repair set. A
filter that turns a fixable candidate into an unfixable one costs more than the
simulations it saves.

2.9 THE GAZE CONTROLLER
-----------------------
Finding: gaze is wired in compiler._compile_composite and NOWHERE ELSE. The grab
path had no head, neck or gaze handling at all -- through an entire pickup the
head sat in its idle pose, and the one moment worth watching, the fingers
closing, was never in view.

gaze_controller.py solves neck and head as a two-link chain, closed-loop against
the pose each frame actually has, splitting error between the joints in
proportion to remaining ROM.

    gaze error  28.1 deg -> 9.1 deg   (saturated=True)

The earlier one-shot aim stalled at 23.5 deg for a structural reason: head
flexion is bounded at 25/-30 deg and a block on a table needs more. Neck and head
together reach what neither reaches alone. saturated=True is honest -- even the
pair cannot fully reach a target that low without the spine, which no state
solves.

Every angle is in the same flexion/abduction/twist DOFs the move vocabulary uses
and clamped to the same rom.v1.json bounds.

2.10 PLANNER-CHOSEN STATES
--------------------------
MotionProgram.motion_states -- one state name per primitive, chosen by the
planner, validated against the closed catalog.

    default (catalog)        first  9.0  mid  9.1  last 56.8
    bot: never look away     first  9.0  mid  9.1  last  9.1
    bot: never look at it    first 56.8  mid 56.8  last 56.8
    invented state rejected: True

The SEQUENCE is the planner's; what a state MEANS stays a reviewed property of
the catalog. A planner that could invent states could invent one that gates
nothing.

Two bugs caught building it:
- program.primitives.index(primitive): equal primitives compare equal, so two
  identical phases both got the first one's state. Now indexed by position.
- The corpus round-trip broke -- 47 committed cases lacked the new field. They
  were re-blessed rather than the guard weakened, and the first pass missed that
  MotionProgram nests itself in `steps`, so 4 sequence cases needed a second
  recursive pass.

2.11 SMALLER FIXES
------------------
- MuJoCo njmax/nconmax, removed in 3.x, replaced with an explicit memory pool.
  The legacy attributes request a large fixed allocation that fails as "engine
  error: Could not allocate memory" under memory pressure, which surfaced as
  intermittent failures in unrelated tests.
- The pipeline dropped every failing candidate, so a truthful rejection was
  indistinguishable from the pipeline breaking. It now nominates the closest
  compiled candidate for inspection, and every candidate card in the UI is
  clickable. Candidates are written to disk whether or not they pass; filtering
  decides what is SELECTED, not what exists.

2026-08-28 -- IT PICKS THE BLOCK UP.  branch grasp/auto-lift
===============================================================================

19.99 cm of lift, held. Two opposing pairs through the carry, thumb at 10 N
against index at 9.3, every fingertip measured outside the block by at least
4.5 mm. Reproducible. src/rigby_poc/scripted_grasp.py is the controller, and it
has no model in the loop on purpose -- it separates two questions this project
confounded for months: whether the BODY can grasp, and whether the selector
chooses well. If it stops lifting after a change, that change broke the body.

Six things had to be true at once, each found by measuring rather than arguing:

1. The thumb closes by ADDUCTION, not flexion. This manifest has recorded it
   from the start -- the thumb is the one chain where the authored asset and the
   anatomical frame disagree, and the rig's own crate_grip preset adducts.
   Closed on its flexion axis the thumb curled without crossing to the fingers.
2. Closure is an absolute, ROM-clamped target, not a delta composed per frame.
   Composing wound up against the rate limiter: opening 8 cm to 19, digits
   anti-parallel -- a hand turning itself inside out while nominally closing.
3. A digit already carrying load stops; the others keep coming. What
   underactuated hands do mechanically, and why they conform to objects they
   were never sized for.
4. Closing stops at the object's own width plus 12 mm. Force alone does not say
   so: the digits read 1-2 N against a 6 N target, kept closing to 4.86 cm
   around a 6.0 cm block, and squeezed it out sideways.
5. The digit welds are springs (solref 0.012, solimp 0.88 0.97). Stiffer and
   they drive through the object -- the thumb reached 13.8 mm INSIDE the block
   and reported 13.85 N of "grip" that was the solver objecting to penetration.
   Softer and the block's own weight levers them open mid-lift.
6. The approach standoff is 1.5 cm, not 4. The palm pad sits at the knuckles
   with the digits reaching most of a hand beyond it, so the standoff decides
   how far INTO the opening the object lands. At 4 cm the block sat out by the
   fingertips and closing pushed it away.

THE PATTERN WORTH KEEPING

Nearly every failure this project has had was a control or a goal that could not
do what its name promised, and the cost was always the same shape: the selector
picks it, correctly, because the situation looks like what the name describes,
and the run is spent. orient_palm took 11 of 26 decisions in 000488 and moved
nothing -- it had already been banned per stage for that, and walked back in the
moment the menu stopped being restricted. thumb_oppose_index took 10 of 26 in
000489 while the C measure sat pinned at its dead value. reach_to took 14 of 26
in 000492 with the block 34 cm away and out of reach.

evals/control_authority.py measures this directly: signed authority of every
primitive over every metric. Run it after touching a primitive. A control that
cannot move a number is worse than a missing one.

WHAT IS NOT DONE

- Generality. It lifts 1 of 7 configurations. Contact and opposing pairs form in
  every one, but only the fitted position carries; the constants were fitted
  against a 6 cm block in one place. A stability gate and a re-approach on
  escape were tried and made it worse; both were reverted rather than compounded.
- The model. The body can grasp; the selector still does not get there. In
  000495 it wrote a correct five-step plan and then spent 19 of 22 calls among
  place_thumb, thumb_home and sweep_thumb, never once calling close_grip. Seven
  primitives and three metrics have since been retired for measured cause.

The shape of it: the model prepares until the clock runs out with the object

changing that. Naming a hard threshold made it worse, not better; 871eaa4 states
both failure modes with their costs instead.

WHAT I WOULD TRY NEXT, IN ORDER

1. Standing targets rather than actions. The model gets ~22 decisions in 14 s,
   about one per 0.64 s; the scripted controller commits to closing within 1.5 s
   of arriving and then needs closure every frame. The target machinery already
   drives greedy at 30 Hz and the model has barely used it -- two of twenty-two
   calls in 000496. A close held as a standing target is the shape that fits.
2. Cadence tied to phase, not to distance. Approaching wants a decision a
   second; the two seconds where a grasp forms want far more, and they get the
   same.
3. Only then, more prompt. Six rounds of it have not moved the outcome.

WHAT CLOSED THE GAP, AND IT WAS NOT PROMPTING

Six runs of prompt edits moved nothing. What moved it was making the BODY refuse
motions whose only available outcome was to undo what had been achieved. Seven
of them, each found by watching a completed grasp being destroyed:

  move_to stops on contact             the arm drove the hand through the block,
                                       thumb 18.9 mm inside, 80 N reported
  move_to stops while holding          approaching what you hold drives the hand
                                       through it (000508)
  move_to stops when the object moves  force never crossed 2.5 N while the
                                       approach shoved the block 9.5 cm (000510)
  close_grip declines beyond 0.038 m   closing on an off-centre object can only
                                       push it (000506, 12 cm in 0.3 s)
  close_grip stops at the object width solids do not compress (4.86 cm around a
                                       6.0 cm block, squeezed out sideways)
  open_grip and sweeps decline while   opening a hand that holds something is
  holding                              how you put it down (000503, 000507)
  lift below 8 N squeezes instead      a grip that is present is not a grip that
                                       carries (000509, dropped at 5.6 N)

The through-line: at one decision every three tenths of a second the model
cannot be responsible for what must be true continuously. Every one of these was
a correct choice for a hand in a slightly different state, and nothing
distinguished the states. A body that refuses the impossible needs no discipline
from the thing deciding.

Two sensing fixes belong with them. Force is the wrong sensor for 'am I pushing
it' -- object_velocity had been in Sensing since the day a still frame could not
tell a resting block from a sliding one, and the approach never read it. And a
request that cannot be honoured is now answered rather than swallowed: a goal on
a retired metric used to report 'reached' on the frame it was set and wake the

2026-08-28 -- CORRECTION: THE GRASP PENETRATES. THE LIFTS ARE NOT VALID.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Angelo saw a finger inside the box in the render and asked for the physics to be
checked. He is right and every penetration check I ran today was measuring the
wrong thing.

I measured fingertip JOINT ORIGINS against the block. The physics collides
CAPSULES along each segment, with radii of 6.5 mm and 7.5 mm, so a finger's
middle can be buried while its tip origin reads clear. Measured properly, against
the capsules the solver actually uses:

    scripted baseline, 16.54 cm lift   deepest -18.2 mm
    run 000511, the VLM's 31.64 cm     deepest  -7.5 mm  (index_2 and index_3)

Eighteen millimetres is a third of the way through a 60 mm block. The claims of
"verified, every fingertip outside the block by 4.5 mm" are wrong, and I made
them twice.

WHY IT PENETRATES, AND WHY TUNING DOES NOT FIX IT

The digits are dynamic bodies welded to commanded poses. The weld pulls a digit
to whatever angle it was given; contact pushes back; the equilibrium sits inside
the object. The holding force IS that overlap. So:

    squeeze 6 N                    -7.5 mm, lifts
    squeeze 11 N                  -12.2 mm, lifts
    contact stiffened             -22.6 mm, lifts   (tunnelling, not stiffness)
    commanded pose stopped at
      the surface                  -3.3 mm, NO LIFT

Every attempt trades penetration against carrying force, because in this
actuation model they are the same quantity. There is no setting that both grips
and stays outside.

One real defect was found on the way: _SQUEEZE_FORCE_N was 6 N while
_CARRY_FORCE_N was 8, so the closure refused to produce the force the lift was
waiting for and lifts happened only on dynamic overshoots. Correcting it to
6/5 made things worse (-17.7 mm, dropped), which means the working configuration
depends on that mismatch. That is not a design, it is a coincidence being relied
upon.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY FIX IT

Torque-controlled digits. Drive the finger joints with actuators that apply a
force against whatever they meet, instead of welding them to a pose and letting
the object argue with the weld. Then grip force is commanded directly and
contact depth is whatever the surface allows -- which is what a grasp is. The
compliant welds added today were half of this idea and the half that does not
resolve it.

Until then: the lifts are real motions of the simulation and are NOT valid
grasps. Do not quote 19.99 or 31.64 cm as a grasp result.
